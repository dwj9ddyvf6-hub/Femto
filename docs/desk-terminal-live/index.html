Words we use

  • Option: a contract giving the right to buy or sell something at an agreed price, by an agreed date.
  • Call: an option that gains in value when the price rises.
  • Put: an option that gains in value when the price falls.
  • Strike: the agreed price at which an option lets you buy or sell.
  • Premium: simply the price paid for an option — or the payment collected when one is sold.
  • Spread: a paired option trade — one option bought and one sold on the same stock — so the most that can be made or lost is capped.
  • Contract: one option contract covers 100 shares.
  • Long: owned — the desk bought it and gains when the price rises.
  • Short: sold first, as a bet the price will fall, and bought back later.
  • Expiry: the date an option runs out.
  • Straddle: buying a call and a put together on the same stock.
  • Wheel: the repeating cycle of selling a put, possibly buying the shares, then selling calls against them.
  • Ladder: options stacked at different prices and dates, like rungs of a ladder.
  • Beta: a measure of how much a stock moves compared with the whole market; high-beta stocks move more.
  • Bar: one period's price summary — where the price opened, its high, its low, and where it closed.
  • ETF: a fund traded like a stock, holding a basket of shares or assets.
  • Tensor: the desk's name for its pattern-scoring engine, which grades stocks for swing setups.
  • VWAP: the day's average traded price, weighted by how much traded at each price.
